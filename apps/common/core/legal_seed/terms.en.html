5. What a certification proves — and what it does not

This is the section to read twice. A GEA certification proves integrity: that a file has not changed since it was registered, and that it existed no later than a given moment. It says nothing about whether what the document claims is true, nor about the authenticity, ownership, value or legal validity of any asset it describes.

In concrete terms: certifying a document that states an asset is worth a certain amount proves that the document said so on that date. It does not verify the amount, the asset, or the claim. The certification record issued with every document says the same thing in its own terms, and that record governs over any summary of it.

Presenting a certified document as if it proved the truth of its contents is a misuse of the service, and Propensiones may cancel a certification and close the account of anyone who does so.

6. Automatic features, stated plainly

Automatic translation

Names and descriptions are translated between Spanish and English by an automated system, without human review. It is a convenience for reading, not a certified or sworn translation, and it may be wrong. Where a text has legal or contractual effect, the version written by its author is the one that governs. Do not rely on a machine translation for a decision that matters.

Time anchoring

Certain documents are anchored in time using a timestamping authority and the Bitcoin blockchain. What is published is only a cryptographic hash — never the document, and never its content. The anchor proves that a given file existed no later than a certain moment. It proves nothing about who wrote it, whether it is true, or what it is worth.

Anchoring in a block is not instantaneous: proofs are issued pending and mature over the following hours. A document is fully usable in the meantime; the anchoring page shows the current state at any time.

12. Governing law and disputes

These terms are governed by Colombian law. Any dispute shall first be raised through the PQRS form, so that it can be resolved directly. If it is not resolved, the parties may attempt conciliation before an authorised conciliation centre before going to court. Failing that, the dispute goes before the ordinary Colombian courts.

Note for review: the arbitration clause does not port.

The standard United States clause — binding individual arbitration before AAA or JAMS, waiver of jury trial, waiver of class actions — is not a matter of swapping the administrator for a local one:

  • There is no jury in Colombian civil procedure, so that waiver means nothing.
  • Group and class actions are governed by Law 472 of 1998 and are of constitutional rank; they are not waived in an adhesion contract.
  • In consumer matters the Superintendence of Industry and Commerce holds jurisdiction under Law 1480 of 2011, and a clause that tries to close that door risks being held abusive and ineffective.
  • Real arbitration here runs under Law 1563 of 2012, before a chamber of commerce arbitration centre, at a cost that only makes sense above a certain amount in dispute.

What is written above is a graduated clause — direct claim, conciliation, then courts — which is the usual arrangement here. If the firm wants arbitration, it belongs in the contracts signed with counterparties for specific transactions, with its seat, rules and amount threshold set out, rather than in the general terms of a platform.
